Effective Diameter – Forward Slip

PyRoll GUI  |  Date: October 2026

1. Introduction

During rolling, the material at the neutral point moves slightly faster than the geometric roll surface speed implied by the working diameter alone — an effect known as forward slip. Converting a simulated strip velocity into a real motor/roll RPM without accounting for this effect systematically overstates the rotational speed the roll actually needs.

PyRoll WebGUI computes an Effective Diameter and the corresponding Forward Slip percentage for every TwoRollPass and ThreeRollPass stand, shown in the Results table directly under Working Diameter. The RPM Basis switch in Pass Design (default: Working Diameter) allows to compute the Roll RPM and Motor RPM from this effective diameter instead.

2. How the Forward Slip Is Calculated

The forward slip is calculated from the equivalent heights of the pass, the working diameter of PyRolL, the classical neutral angle and a temperature-based friction. All inputs are available from the simulation, no measurement or CAD drawing is needed.

2.1 Reduced zone and equivalent heights

The entry and the exit contour of the pass are laid over each other. The zone in which the entry profile is thicker than the exit profile is the part of the bar that is actually reduced. It is bounded by the two points where the contours cross (for a round bar in an oval groove these are the points where the bar outline meets the groove arcs). Only this strip counts: its width is \(b_l\), and the equivalent heights are the profile areas inside the strip divided by \(b_l\):

\[ h_{0,\text{eq}} = \frac{A_{0m}}{b_l}, \qquad h_{1,\text{eq}} = \frac{A_{1m}}{b_l}, \qquad \Delta h = h_{0,\text{eq}} - h_{1,\text{eq}} \]

The strip is found automatically from the calculated entry and exit cross-sections, so the method works for any groove type (round, oval, box, square, diamond, …).

For ThreeRollPass stands the strip method is not applicable (the rolls are arranged at 120°). There \(h_{0,\text{eq}}\) and \(h_{1,\text{eq}}\) are the equivalent heights of the entry and exit profile calculated by PyRolL. This variant has not yet been validated against measured data.

2.2 Forward slip and effective diameter

\[ D_w = 2 \cdot r_w, \qquad x = \frac{\Delta h}{2\,D_w}, \qquad \xi = \sqrt{x} - \frac{x}{\mu} \] \[ \text{FWS} = 0.75 \cdot \frac{\xi^2}{2} \cdot \left(\frac{D_w}{h_{1,\text{eq}}} - 1\right) \] \[ D_{\text{eff}} = D_w \cdot (1 + \text{FWS}), \qquad \text{GF} = D - D_{\text{eff}}, \qquad n = \frac{60\,000 \cdot v}{\pi \cdot D_{\text{eff}}} \]
SymbolMeaning
\(D\)Nominal roll diameter (Roll Diameter in the Results table)
\(r_w\)Working radius of PyRolL
\(v\)Exit speed of the bar [m/s]
\(\mu\)Friction used for the forward slip, see section 3
\(D_w\)Working diameter of PyRolL ("Working Diameter" row of the Results table)
GFGroove Factor: nominal roll diameter minus effective diameter [mm]. This is the deduction a mill control that only receives the nominal diameter has to apply to obtain the effective diameter
\(n\)Roll speed [rpm] when the effective diameter is used as RPM basis
The effective diameter is always the Working Diameter row of the Results table × (1 + FWS), so it is never smaller than the Working Diameter. The Groove Factor row is the nominal roll diameter minus the effective diameter, not PyRolL's internal groove factor.

3. Friction

The friction used for the forward slip depends on the bar temperature, the roll diameter and the roll speed. It is independent of the Coulomb friction coefficient of the pass, which continues to be used for roll force, torque and spreading:

\[ \mu = Q_1 \cdot Q_2 \cdot (1.05 - 0.0005\,T) \]
FactorValue
\(T\)Mean bar temperature at the entry of the pass, per stand, in °C
\(Q_1\) (roll diameter)0.8 for \(D < 250\) mm, otherwise 0.9 (for \(T \ge 400\) °C; below 400 °C \(Q_1 = 1\) for \(D \ge 250\) mm)
\(Q_2\) (roll speed)1 for \(D < 250\) mm or \(v \le 2\) m/s, otherwise \(0.4 + 0.6\,e^{-0.2\,(v-2)}\)

A colder bar has a higher friction and therefore a higher forward slip; a hotter bar the opposite.

4. The "RPM Basis" Switch

Pass Design offers a per-sequence RPM Basis selector (next to Disk Element Count):

OptionEffect
Working Diameter (default)Roll RPM / Motor RPM computed from the PyRolL working diameter.
Effective DiameterRoll RPM / Motor RPM, the ideal RPM (Roll RPM and Motor RPM, zero tension) and the gear tension of Finishing Blocks are computed from the forward-slip-corrected effective diameter (section 2). Stands without a forward slip result use the working diameter.
Scope: the switch applies to all stands whose velocity is given or solved (Standard Solve with velocity input, Forward and Backward Solve), including Finishing Block sub-passes, whose velocities follow from the entered start/end velocity and the gear ratios. Only a Finishing Block motor RPM prescribed in Standard Solve is used as entered — that RPM is a hard physical input, not something derived from a simulated velocity that forward slip could correct.
Does not affect simulated rolling velocity: PyRolL's own mass-conservation solve always uses the working diameter internally to relate velocity and rotational frequency. This switch only changes how an already-simulated, unchanged velocity is converted into a displayed RPM figure — it does not feed back into profile dimensions, roll force, torque, power, or temperature results. The power and torque values themselves stay PyRolL's values; only the tension-corrected powers use the displayed RPM and are therefore lower by about the forward slip when the effective diameter is the RPM basis.

The Engine Evaluation uses the Roll RPM of the Results and therefore follows the RPM Basis. The default is the Working Diameter. We recommend validating the Effective Diameter against real measured motor RPM for your own mill, since forward slip depends strongly on friction, reduction, and contact geometry that vary between mills.

5. Scope and Validity

  • The forward slip is calculated for TwoRollPass and ThreeRollPass stands. For Asymmetric Roll Passes the Results table shows "—" and the RPM basis falls back to the working diameter.
  • If the entry profile is not thicker than the exit profile (no reduction), the result is left undefined ("—"). The forward slip is never negative.
  • The factors of the method were established for round – oval – round wire rod rolling. Other groove shapes (box, square, diamond, …) and Three-Roll passes use the same formulas but have not yet been verified against measured data. Please compare with your own mill data before relying on them operationally.
  • PyRolL's built-in neutral-angle model (Lippmann-Mahrenholz) is not used for the forward slip: it does not use friction directly and gave roughly ten times lower forward slip than the values measured in rolling mills. It is still used for the tension-aware force and torque calculations.
